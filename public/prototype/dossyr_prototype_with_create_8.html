Step 3 of 6

Consent, in plain language.

Three things to confirm before your session, each one confirmed individually. The full legal version lives in the Privacy Policy, but you shouldn't need a lawyer to understand what happens here.

1
Your profile data and your rights over it
Your simulation produces a DMCS profile. Here's exactly how that data is treated:
●You own it. Your DMCS profile is portable. It travels with you across every employer on Dossyr.
●It's used for two things only: building your verified profile, and benchmarking it fairly against others at your tier.
●Export or delete at any time. Deleting your account removes your personal information. Your score record is anonymized.
●You control sharing. Employers see your Prooffile only on your terms, and you can view it exactly as they see it.
✓
I understand how my profile data is used, and my right to export or delete it.
2
Integrity monitoring during your session
Dossyr checks the integrity of the assessment itself, so that your verified score actually means something to employers. Here is what is and isn't checked:
●Checked: behavior patterns inside the assessment window only: paste events and improbable response timing.
✕Never checked: no audio, no screen recording, no keystroke logging outside the session, no eye tracking.
●If something is flagged, you get a plain-language explanation and the option of a supervised reassessment. There is never a public penalty on your profile.
✓
I understand what integrity monitoring checks during my session, and what it never checks.
3
Proof Capture: your choice, either way
Short face clips at a handful of notable moments during your session. That's all it is. No audio. No screen. Never the full session. Clips are never used for scoring, ranking, or training AI. They exist only to give employers visual confidence that you are the person behind your score.
Recommended
Enable Proof Capture
Adds visual verification to your Prooffile. You control sharing per employer, and you can delete your clips at any time.
Fully supported
Opt out
Shown neutrally to employers as "Proof Capture not shared." No effect on your score, your DMCS profile, or your discovery eligibility.
Confirm items 1 and 2, and make your Proof Capture choice, to continue. You can change your Proof Capture choice before every session. Full details: Privacy Policy · Evidence Capture Policy.

You won't work alone. Across your scenarios you'll encounter AI teammates, each with a distinct personality, priorities, and communication style. What your scenarios may draw from

Your session adapts as you go. Which scenarios you see, and how they branch, responds to your decisions. They're drawn from seven families that together cover the reality of the role:

🔍 Debugging & Diagnosis ⚖️ Prioritization & Tradeoffs 🌫️ Ambiguity Handling 🗺️ Planning & Decomposition 🤝 Collaboration & Alignment 🛡️ Quality & Risk ⏱️ Pressure & Time Constraints
How this works
✓
There are no trick questions. Scenarios reflect real situations. We're measuring how you think and work, not whether you guess a hidden answer.
✓
You can pause. Within reasonable limits, you can step away for connectivity or accessibility needs. There's no countdown clock designed to create panic, just a progress indicator.
✓
You'll know when you're done. The session ends with a clear completion moment, not an abrupt cutoff, once your scenario coverage is complete.
✓
Proof Capture is on. Short face clips at a few notable moments. No audio, no screen, never the full session. You control sharing afterward.
Once you begin, your first scenario loads. You can pause, but the session runs as one continuous experience.

// A rate limiter I built for a personal API project // Sliding window algorithm — started simple, got interesting class RateLimiter { constructor(windowMs, maxRequests) { this.windowMs = windowMs; this.maxRequests = maxRequests; this.requests = new Map(); } isAllowed(clientId) { const now = Date.now(); const windowStart = now - this.windowMs; // Clean up old timestamps if (this.requests.has(clientId)) { const timestamps = this.requests.get(clientId) .filter(t => t > windowStart); this.requests.set(clientId, timestamps); } const current = this.requests.get(clientId) || []; if (current.length >= this.maxRequests) { return false; } current.push(now); this.requests.set(clientId, current); return true; } getRemainingRequests(clientId) { const current = this.requests.get(clientId) || []; return Math.max(0, this.maxRequests - current.length); } }

How is this verified?
Dossyr makes specific claims about candidate capability and session integrity. This page explains exactly how those claims are produced, what they mean, and how you can inspect them. Nothing here is a marketing assertion — it is a description of the system.
🧪
What WSE measures — and how
▼
The Workplace Simulation Engine (WSE) is a scenario-based simulation, not a quiz or a trivia test. Candidates work through realistic engineering scenarios — debugging production systems, handling competing priorities, responding to ambiguous requirements, designing under constraints. The scenarios are written to reflect how engineering work actually happens.

What WSE measures: behavioral signals — how a candidate reasons, how they approach uncertainty, how they adapt when new information arrives, whether they ask the right questions. Not whether they know specific syntax or recall a definition.

What WSE does not test: the content of any specific answer. There is no single correct solution. The behavioral pattern across multiple scenarios is what produces the DMCS profile.

Adaptive delivery (ARISE): scenario sequencing and difficulty are adjusted in real time based on how the candidate responds. This makes the session harder to game — the same script does not repeat across candidates, and static preparation is less effective than genuine capability.
📊
What the DMCS profile means
▼
The DMCS (Dynamic Multi-dimensional Capability Score) is a five-dimension behavioral profile produced by Dossyr's scoring system after the WSE session is complete. Scores are benchmarked against all candidates who have completed the same domain and tier — not against an absolute scale.

The five dimensions:
  • TAI — Technical Ability Index: applied technical reasoning, hypothesis formation, signal extraction from noisy data
  • PR — Problem Resolution: diagnosis quality, decision tradeoffs, reaching workable outcomes under incomplete information
  • IS — Information Synthesis: extracting relevant signal from ambiguous inputs, prioritization clarity
  • SA — Situational Awareness: awareness of team dependencies, second-order consequences, cross-functional risk
  • LAM — Learning and Adaptation Mindset: calibration quality, responsiveness to new information, behavior when initial approach fails
Scores are shown as strength bands (Developing / Proficient / Advanced / Distinguished) — not as raw numbers. This is intentional: bands are more interpretable than percentiles and less gameable.

What DMCS does not measure: school, company history, self-presentation, or anything from a resume. The profile is derived entirely from behavioral signals in the WSE session.
🛡
What CIQ checks — and what each status means
▼
The Candidate Integrity Quotient (CIQ) is an independent integrity layer that runs throughout the WSE session. It does not access the content of a candidate's answers. It monitors session-level behavioral signals — timing patterns, input consistency, environmental signals — and produces a confidence indicator.

CIQ does not accuse candidates of cheating. It provides a signal that employers can factor into their own judgment alongside the DMCS.

StatusMeaning
CleanNo significant anomalies. Session proceeded within expected behavioral parameters across all signal categories.
ReviewOne or more signals warrant closer attention. DMCS is valid. Employer should factor in the flag and may request a supervised reassessment.
CompromisedMultiple or significant anomalies detected. Confidence in the DMCS as a reliable signal is materially reduced. Dossyr recommends supervised reassessment before this profile is used as a primary shortlisting signal.
CIQ does not modify DMCS scores. They are independent outputs. An employer sees both and makes their own judgment.
📹
What Proof Capture captures — and what it explicitly does not
▼
Proof Capture is opt-in. Candidates who enable it allow Dossyr to capture short, selective face clips at specific moments during the WSE session — triggered by the ARISE engine, not recorded continuously.

What Proof Capture captures:
  • Face-only clips (no audio, no screen)
  • Three to six short clips per session, each a few seconds
  • An identity anchor at session start and a completion anchor at session end
  • ARISE-triggered "notable moment" clips during high-signal scenarios
What Proof Capture does not capture:
  • No continuous recording — clips only, not a session video
  • No audio under any circumstances
  • No screen content, keystrokes, or environment scanning
  • No biometric data beyond confirming a face is present
Candidate controls: Proof Capture sharing is off by default for each employer. Candidates enable it explicitly, per employer, and can revoke access at any time. Deletion removes clips within 24 hours.
🔑
The Assessment ID and audit trail
▼
Every completed WSE session generates a unique Assessment ID. This ID ties the DMCS, CIQ status, Proof Capture clips, and session metadata together into a permanent, auditable record.
Assessment ID format
DSY-[domain]-[tier]-[date]-[unique hash]
Example: DSY-SWE-2-20260612-a94f
What the Assessment ID guarantees:
  • The score was generated by Dossyr's system from a real session
  • The score has not been modified after generation (immutable record)
  • The session version (WSE / ARISE / DMCS) is locked to the ID — comparability is trackable
  • Disputes and supervised reassessments reference this ID explicitly
Employers can request a detailed audit report from Dossyr (5–10 business day SLA). The report includes event summary, signal aggregates, and CIQ reasoning — not raw response content.
